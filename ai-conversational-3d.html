1. The modelling workflow

Choose your starting method

Photo-to-3D: supply photographs of accessible sides, corners, openings and structural connections, with measurements and scale references where available. Prompt-to-3D: describe the desired object or environment, including geometry, proportions, dimensions, materials and features. Hybrid: combine photographs with instructions for additions or changes. Both methods lead into the same modelling, inspection and revision workflow.

Interpret

For photographs, ask AI to inventory visible surfaces, geometry, junctions, materials and uncertainties. For text prompts, ask AI to translate requirements into an explicit modelling specification and identify missing decisions. Require separation of directly observed features from inferred depth or dimensions.

Construct

Build a first model using supported software. Preserve reference images when supplied, or the originating text specification for prompt-based models. Identify which geometry is provisional.

Inspect

Rotate the model and check walls, faces, openings, alignment, scale, occlusion and camera perspective. Test visibility from multiple angles; watch for walls turning transparent or disappearing.

Correct

Describe one defect precisely, including expected and actual behaviour. Request a bounded correction without altering verified geometry elsewhere.

Verify and preserve

Compare before and after, log what changed, record outstanding uncertainty and preserve a version reference. For photo reconstruction, verify against measurements and references; for prompt-generated designs, verify against the agreed specification. Do not equate design conformity with real-world measurement accuracy.

5. Recommended conversation protocol

Use screenshots with your text instructions. A screenshot of the current 3D model provides immediate visual context: the camera angle, visible geometry, missing components, material defects and the exact problem you are describing. Annotate or describe the affected area when necessary.

Screenshot → describe → correct → screenshot again. Capture the model, explain the observed issue in natural language, request a targeted change, then provide a new screenshot to compare the result. Use multiple viewpoints when a defect may depend on camera angle, visibility, face orientation or clipping.

Keep the scope of each change narrow. Ask the AI to distinguish what it observed in the screenshot, what it inferred, what it changed, what it could not verify, and what must be checked next. A screenshot documents a particular visible view; it does not prove hidden geometry, measurements or construction accuracy. Do not allow an attractive render to substitute for geometric validation.

When resuming a different conversation, supply the latest model, the original photographs or text specification (or both), relevant screenshots showing previous problems and corrections, and the most recent continuity snapshot. Without these materials, the AI may not reliably reconstruct earlier model state.
