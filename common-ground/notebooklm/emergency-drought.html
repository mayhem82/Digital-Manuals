Drought & water shortage

Plain HTML study copy for importing into NotebookLM or another reading tool. Current official warnings and emergency-service directions take priority.

Track supply before it becomes urgent. A water budget helps you decide when to act.

Urgent action: Prioritise safe drinking water and animal welfare. Never assume tank, dam or bore water is safe because it looks clear.

Before

  1. List water sources and usable volumes, including reserve limits.
  2. Estimate household and livestock demand and losses.
  3. Fix leaks through suitable trades and plan safe backup supply early.
  4. Set a review date and supply trigger with your household or farm adviser.

During

  1. Check reserves regularly and update the budget as demand changes.
  2. Follow local restrictions and keep drinking and non-potable water separate.
  3. Seek agricultural advice early on feed, stocking levels and water cartage.
  4. Maintain fire and flash-flood readiness even in dry conditions.

After

  1. Inspect catchments and storage before relying on replenished water.
  2. Seek NSW Health advice after contamination, flood or bushfire.
  3. Use alternative safe water when contamination is suspected.
  4. Review how long supplies lasted and improve the next budget.

Country properties

Include all stock, evaporation, leakage and pump dependence. Boiling does not remove chemical or fire-retardant contamination.
