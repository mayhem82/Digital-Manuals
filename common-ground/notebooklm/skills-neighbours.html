Helping a neighbour

Plain HTML study copy for importing into NotebookLM or another reading tool. Current official warnings and emergency-service directions take priority.

Practical support, led by the person.

Start with the person, not the task

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • A conversation and consent

Steps

  1. Ask what help the person wants and what they prefer to manage themselves.
  2. Agree boundaries, access and how to contact them.
  3. Write only the details they want shared and review regularly.

Know the limit: Age or disability does not tell you what someone can do.

  • Person-Centred Emergency Preparedness

Do an agreed property walk-through

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Owner-led map
  • notebook

Steps

  1. Ask the person to show important paths, gates and equipment safely.
  2. Record what each item does and who normally maintains it.
  3. Choose small agreed tasks and flag anything needing specialist help.

Know the limit: Do not enter private areas or operate unfamiliar equipment.

  • Person-Centred Emergency Preparedness

Keep paths and entrances accessible

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Person guidance
  • suitable hand tools

Steps

  1. Ask which route and surface work for their mobility aid.
  2. Move agreed loose items and keep thresholds, ramps and gates clear.
  3. Report lighting, drainage or surface problems for suitable repair.

Know the limit: Do not alter ramps, rails or access layouts without agreement and competent advice.

Plan around medical power needs

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Person
  • provider and support network

Steps

  1. Ask what support they want if electricity stops.
  2. Have their equipment provider confirm backup time and safe alternatives.
  3. Agree an early relocation plan and transport that meets their needs.

Know the limit: Do not change medical equipment, oxygen settings or treatment.

  • NSW power outages

Help with animals by agreement

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Written care instructions

Steps

  1. Ask about animal behaviour, feeding, water and containment.
  2. Complete only tasks demonstrated and agreed with the owner.
  3. Have a backup handler, veterinary contact and emergency destination.

Know the limit: Do not enter with unfamiliar large animals or improvise medication.

Agree safe transport and evacuation

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Person
  • carer or provider where invited

Steps

  1. Ask what vehicle, communication, mobility aids and support are needed.
  2. Arrange early transport and an accessible destination.
  3. Agree who calls whom if the primary helper is unavailable.

Know the limit: Do not promise a rescue or lift/transfer a person without appropriate training.

  • Person-Centred Emergency Preparedness

Support without lifting or transferring

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Person directions
  • trained support contact

Steps

  1. Ask how you can assist without moving their body.
  2. Offer agreed practical help such as clearing access or carrying supplies.
  3. Contact trained support for transfers, hoists or personal care.

Know the limit: After a fall or injury, do not attempt an improvised lift; seek appropriate help.

Create a neighbour support agreement

Skill area: Helping a neighbour. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Simple written plan

Steps

  1. List the agreed tasks, visit times and contact method.
  2. Add backup helpers, no-response steps and emergency triggers.
  3. Review together after a change and keep each person copy accessible.

Know the limit: Keep keys, codes and health details private; never promise availability you cannot provide.
