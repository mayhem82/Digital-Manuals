Emergency readiness

Plain HTML study copy for importing into NotebookLM or another reading tool. Current official warnings and emergency-service directions take priority.

Small preparations with a big purpose.

Pack a practical emergency kit

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Waterproof bag or container

Steps

  1. Put together water, food, torch, radio and first-aid supplies.
  2. Add documents, keys, cash, medication needs and accessibility items.
  3. Keep it accessible and review contents each season.

Know the limit: Plan enough for your household and possible rural isolation.

  • NSW SES emergency kit

Write a leave-early plan

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Paper plan
  • local hazard advice

Steps

  1. Agree the trigger, destination, transport and two possible routes.
  2. Include animals, accessibility, carers and backup help.
  3. Rehearse before dangerous weather and keep a printed copy.

Know the limit: Read live warnings: when leaving is unsafe, follow official shelter directions.

  • NSW Rural Fire Service

Practise a house fire escape

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Working alarms
  • household agreement

Steps

  1. Plan usable exits, a meeting place and help arrangements.
  2. Practise in normal conditions without blocking exits.
  3. If fire occurs, get out, stay out and call 000.

Know the limit: Do not re-enter for people, animals or possessions; tell firefighters.

  • Fire and Rescue NSW escape plans

Prepare for a flood warning

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Local flood advice
  • property map

Steps

  1. Learn which roads and parts of the property flood first.
  2. Agree an early departure or isolation plan with suitable supplies.
  3. Move essential items and animals early while routes are safe.

Know the limit: Never drive, ride, walk or play in floodwater.

  • NSW SES

Prepare loose items for high winds

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Owner agreement
  • suitable storage

Steps

  1. Identify outdoor furniture, tools, bins and loose equipment.
  2. Store or secure them to their instructions before severe weather.
  3. Arrange professional attention to damaged trees or roofing.

Know the limit: Do not go outside to recover items once conditions become dangerous.

  • NSW SES

Make a drought water budget

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Tank records
  • household and animal demand

Steps

  1. List sources and usable stored volumes.
  2. Estimate daily demand and losses with an agricultural adviser where needed.
  3. Set a reorder point and arrange safe backup supply early.

Know the limit: Keep potable and non-potable water separate.

  • NSW farm water planning

Make a heat-day plan

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Cool destination
  • transport contacts

Steps

  1. Identify the coolest suitable place and a backup location.
  2. Agree check-ins and transport before hot weather.
  3. Reduce exertion and follow medical advice on fluids and medication storage.

Know the limit: Confusion or collapse can be heatstroke: call 000 and follow dispatcher advice.

  • NSW Health heat guidance

Plan a safe return after a disaster

Skill area: Emergency readiness. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Official all-clear
  • repair contacts

Steps

  1. Wait until authorities permit return and the route is safe.
  2. Look for damage from a safe position before entering.
  3. Arrange professional electrical, structural and water assessments as needed.

Know the limit: An all-clear for the area does not certify your building or equipment safe.

  • NSW SES
