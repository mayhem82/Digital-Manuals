Taps, tanks, leaks and country water.

Find your household water shut-off

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Property plan
  • torch

Steps

  1. Ask the owner or plumber to identify the correct valve.
  2. Record its location and which supply it controls.
  3. Check access stays clear and arrange a demonstration in normal conditions.

Know the limit: Do not force a seized valve or operate shared infrastructure without agreement.

Check for a hidden water leak

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Meter access
  • notebook

Steps

  1. Turn off normal water uses when safe, including automatic ones.
  2. Record the meter reading and recheck after a quiet interval.
  3. Unexpected use or a damp patch is a reason to contact a plumber.

Know the limit: Check for livestock, irrigation or shared supplies before assuming a leak.

Clean a removable tap aerator

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Tap manual
  • cloth

Steps

  1. Confirm the aerator is user-removable and protect the sink drain.
  2. Remove gently as directed and rinse debris from the mesh.
  3. Reassemble in order and check for leaks.

Know the limit: Stop if it is stuck; do not wrench against the tap or pipework.

Respond to an overflowing toilet

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Gloves
  • known isolation tap

Steps

  1. Stop flushing and keep children and animals away.
  2. Close the accessible toilet isolation tap if safe and familiar.
  3. Arrange a plumber and clean contaminated areas with suitable protection.

Know the limit: Sewage backups or repeated blockages need professional assessment.

Map a rainwater system

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Tank plan
  • notebook

Steps

  1. Trace roof catchment, first-flush device, tank, pump and outlets from outside.
  2. Label drinking and non-drinking outlets accurately.
  3. Record service contacts and inspection dates.

Know the limit: Never enter a tank; water that looks clear can still be unsafe.

  • NSW Health rainwater guidance

Check a tank level safely

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • External gauge or approved monitor

Steps

  1. Read the external level indicator without climbing or opening the tank.
  2. Estimate usable capacity from tank information, allowing for inaccessible volume.
  3. Log weekly use and arrange supply early when reserves fall.

Know the limit: Keep tank lids secured; children and animals can fall into openings.

  • NSW farm water planning

Recognise a pump problem

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Pump manual
  • service contact

Steps

  1. Note unusual noise, loss of flow or repeated cycling.
  2. Check only user-level indicators and safe external supply conditions.
  3. Stop using the pump as its manual directs and call a technician.

Know the limit: Do not run a dry pump, bypass controls or touch flooded electrical equipment.

  • NSW Health rainwater guidance

Understand your septic system

Skill area: Water & plumbing. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Service records
  • site plan

Steps

  1. Locate the tank and absorption area from plans or a technician.
  2. Keep vehicles and building work away from the system.
  3. Record service schedules and report odour, ponding or slow drains.

Know the limit: Never open or enter a septic tank; hazardous gases can kill.
