NSW emergency preparation overview

In a life-threatening emergency or fire, call 000. For NSW flood, storm or tsunami emergency help, call NSW SES 132 500. Current local warnings and emergency-service instructions take priority.

Warning levels

  • Advice: stay informed.
  • Watch and Act: take protective action now.
  • Emergency Warning: immediate danger; act immediately.
  • Read the action in the warning. It may say evacuate, or shelter because leaving is unsafe.

Telephone warning messages

Emergency Alert can send recorded landline calls and SMS a warning area. Keep provider address details current. A switched-off phone, no reception or power loss can prevent delivery; NBN landlines may fail during an outage.

Accessible emergency calls

Ordinary SMS to 000 or 106 is not supported. 106 is the TTY emergency service. People who are Deaf, hard of hearing or have speech communication difficulty can prepare an official National Relay Service emergency option.

NRS emergency options and SMS Relay

UHF on country properties

Learn the owner agreed channel and call signs. UHF CB emergency channels 5/35 do not guarantee rescue and are not a substitute 000. Emergency services do not routinely monitor them.

Give a clear emergency report

Say FIRE, POLICE or AMBULANCE; give the town, street address and correct gate. Explain what happened and who needs help, including access or communication needs. Stay on the line and follow the operator.

Home & small repairs

The little fixes that make a home work.

Tighten a loose cupboard handle

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Matching screwdriver

Steps

  1. Open the door and support the handle.
  2. Seat the correct screwdriver fully in the screw head.
  3. Tighten gently, then check movement; stop if the screw spins.

Know the limit: A split door or stripped fixing needs a suitable repair, not more force.

Measure a space accurately

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Tape measure
  • paper
  • pencil

Steps

  1. Sketch the space and label width, height and depth.
  2. Measure each dimension twice in the same units.
  3. Record clearances for doors, hinges and fittings.

Know the limit: Check the tape hook is seated; allow for uneven walls.

Stop a door from slamming

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Door buffer or soft closer

Steps

  1. Find the contact point with the door moving slowly.
  2. Choose a removable buffer compatible with the surface.
  3. Fit to its instructions and check the door still closes safely.

Know the limit: Do not alter fire doors, required self-closers or emergency exits.

Clean an appliance filter

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Appliance manual
  • soft brush

Steps

  1. Find the user-serviceable filter in the manual.
  2. Switch off and unplug if instructed; let the appliance cool.
  3. Clean as directed, dry fully where required and refit securely.

Know the limit: Do not remove service panels or reach into internal electrical parts.

Fix a squeaky hinge

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Compatible lubricant
  • cloth

Steps

  1. Identify the squeaky hinge and protect the floor.
  2. Apply a small amount of the recommended lubricant.
  3. Move the door slowly and wipe excess away.

Know the limit: Support heavy doors; leave damaged or fire-door hinges to a professional.

Fit removable draught sealing

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Tape measure
  • suitable sealing strip

Steps

  1. Find the draught and check the gap with the door closed.
  2. Clean and dry the contact surface.
  3. Fit the strip to instructions and check easy opening and latching.

Know the limit: Never block required ventilation, weep holes or appliance air supply.

Inspect a roof from the ground

Skill area: Home & small repairs. Level: Beginner friendly.

Have to hand

  • Binoculars
  • phone camera

Steps

  1. Stand on stable ground clear of traffic and powerlines.

Bushfire & grassfire

Leaving early is the safest option. Make the decision before fire threatens your route.

Urgent action: Follow the action in the current NSW RFS warning. If it is too late to leave, follow official shelter advice. Call 000 if life is threatened.

Before

  1. Write your leave-early triggers, destination, routes and backup transport.
  2. Prepare the property well ahead of dangerous fire weather; arrange qualified help for high-risk work.
  3. Plan early animal relocation and accessible support. Keep your kit ready.
  4. Check NSW RFS fire danger ratings, bans and current incidents.

During

  1. Leave early while routes are safe; do not delay for possessions or maintenance.
  2. Read the current warning action. An Emergency Warning may say evacuate or shelter.
  3. If told it is too late to leave, follow official shelter directions.
  4. Do not return for possessions or animals; tell emergency services if someone is missing.

After

  1. Return only when authorities permit and the route is safe.
  2. Stay clear of damaged buildings, fallen trees and powerlines.
  3. Have damaged electrical systems and pumps assessed before use.
  4. Use safe alternative drinking water if tanks or catchments may be contaminated.

Country properties

Put gate directions, access limitations and animal transport arrangements in your plan. Tank water and pumps may be unavailable during an outage.

Flood & flash flooding

Know what floods first. Leave low-lying areas early when advised and while routes are safe.

Urgent action: Never drive, ride, walk or play in floodwater. If trapped or in life-threatening danger, call 000.

Before

  1. Learn local flood risk and which roads, bridges and access tracks close first.
  2. Agree safe routes, an early departure trigger and a destination.
  3. Move animals and essential items early; plan supplies for possible isolation.
  4. Arrange help ahead of time for mobility, transport and power needs.

During

  1. Follow NSW SES instructions; leave when advised while routes are safe.
  2. Do not enter floodwater, including to check its depth or rescue possessions.
  3. Avoid wet electrical equipment and do not attempt to operate flooded pumps.
  4. If trapped, call 000, give your exact location and follow operator advice.

After

  1. Wait for official permission to return.
  2. Have flooded wiring, appliances and pumps professionally checked before use.
  3. Avoid contaminated water, unstable buildings and damaged septic systems.
  4. Do not use flood-contaminated tank water; obtain safe water and NSW Health advice.

Thunderstorms, lightning & hail

Plan for lightning, hail, damaging winds and intense rain together. Thunderstorms can also produce sudden flash flooding; a storm does not have to be classified as severe to pose a lightning risk.

At thunder or lightning, stop outdoor work and move into safe enclosed shelter. Do not wait for rain or an app notification. Call 000 for a lightning strike, fire or immediate danger.

Before a thunderstorm

  1. Check BOM forecasts and warnings for the property's locality. Read the issue time, affected places and expected hazards; check NSW SES advice and Live Traffic before deciding to travel.
  2. Write down the substantial enclosed building you will use and the clear route to its door. Include the resident's mobility aid, assistance animal and agreed helper.
  3. Set an early finish time for fencing, mowing, paddock and water jobs when storms are forecast. Allow travel time to shelter instead of waiting for the first nearby lightning.
  4. In calm conditions, store loose lightweight items, park vehicles under sound cover away from trees and low ground, and bring pets indoors. Do not plan a last-minute animal chase.
  5. Charge phones and approved medical backups. Put torches, the battery radio, spare batteries and paper contacts together. Agree a neighbour check-in that will not require travel during the storm.
  6. Mark low crossings, creek approaches and stormwater routes on the property map. Arrange earlier departure if access may flood, while the route is still safe.

During the storm

  1. Move into a substantial enclosed building as soon as thunder is heard or lightning seen. Avoid isolated trees, open sheds, verandahs, picnic shelters, metal fences and exposed high ground.
  2. Stay away from windows and doors during hail. Avoid corded phones, plugged-in electrical equipment and plumbing such as showers and taps while lightning is nearby.
  3. If sheltering in an enclosed metal-roofed vehicle, close the windows and avoid touching metal parts. Stop safely away from trees, powerlines, creeks and low ground; never drive into floodwater.
  4. Keep listening for official updates. Stay sheltered rather than going outside to save a car from hail, retrieve washing, clear a drain or repair a roof.

After thunder and lightning

  1. Remain in safe lightning shelter for at least 30 minutes after the last thunder. Restart the waiting period if thunder returns; other storm hazards or official directions may require longer sheltering.
  2. From a safe position, look for broken glass, damaged roofing, hanging branches and fallen wires. Assume fallen powerlines and objects touching them are live; keep away.
  3. Do not enter floodwater or use wet electrical equipment. Arrange qualified assessment of damaged wiring, pumps and structures. Photograph damage only when the location is safe.
  4. Use the agreed phone check-in with neighbours. Call NSW SES 132 500 for storm or flood emergency assistance, and 000 for injuries, lightning strikes or life-threatening conditions.
  5. Record what failed and which route or shelter was difficult to use. Replace depleted batteries and revise the early finish time before the next forecast storm.

On a country property

Decide stock care, access, pump and tank arrangements before a storm. An open farm shed is not suitable lightning shelter. Move people to shelter first; a brief quiet interval between storm cells is not an all-clear.

Practise in normal conditions

Walk the agreed route to shelter with the resident's consent. Check the door, mobility-aid space, radio reception and paper contacts. Record anything that would delay reaching shelter.
